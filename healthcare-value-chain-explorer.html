The healthcare value chain, and where Skedulo sits

How care at home is funded, delivered, proven and paid for in the US, Australia, the UK, Canada and ASEAN, and which links depend on Skedulo.

Skedulo turns funded care into visits, and its records decide who gets paid

Care outside hospitals moves through eight stages, from funding to the worker's pay. Upstream systems hold the money, the client record and the care plan. Downstream systems run claims and payroll. Skedulo owns rostering and the visit itself, and the actual times it records feed both claims and pay.

What changes by region

  • United StatesMany payers. Medicaid visits must pass Electronic Visit Verification, and states began denying unmatched claims in 2026.
  • AustraliaTwo national funders (NDIA and Services Australia) and the SCHADS Award. Skedulo's deepest product: the AU Healthcare package with Lumary and payroll.
  • United KingdomCouncils and the NHS buy most care. CQC, NHS digital standards and UK GDPR shape the stack.
  • CanadaProvinces contract providers; in Ontario one agency coordinates over 150 of them.
  • ASEANSingapore has a funded system much like Australia's. Elsewhere, families and private payers still carry most care.

Why demand keeps growing

People want to age at home, home is cheaper than a nursing home for most needs, and populations are ageing fast. Funding and workers are the brakes, so waiting lists grow. Providers have to deliver more care per worker hour, which is the value Skedulo sells.

Demand side: who receives care, why, and who pays

The end user is the person receiving care at home or in the community, often with a family member arranging it. They rarely pay the full cost: governments and insurers pay most, and the client pays a share or tops it up. Need grows faster than funding and the workforce, so budgets and staff set how much care is delivered.

Who the end users are

Why they choose care at home

  • PreferenceIn AARP's 2024 survey, 75% of US adults aged 50+ want to stay in their home as they age, and 73% in their community.
  • CostUS 2025 medians: $35 an hour for a non-medical caregiver ($80,080 a year at 44 hours a week) against $129,575 a year for a private nursing-home room.
  • PolicyFunders steer care home. Australia's Support at Home came out of the Royal Commission into Aged Care; England pools council and NHS money in the Better Care Fund.
  • DemographyUS households led by people aged 80+ will double by 2040. About one in five Australians will be over 65 by 2063. Every ASEAN state will be ageing, aged or super-aged by 2050.

What keeps demand growing, and what holds it back

NDIS participants reached 782,013 in June 2026 (+6% in a year). 106,977 older Australians waited for a Support at Home place on 30 June 2026. About 650,000 people sat on US Medicaid home-care waiting lists in KFF's 2026 survey.

Does anyone cover the whole chain?

No. Funding belongs to governments and insurers, and only in the US does one group own both the insurer and the providers. Three kinds of player come closest.
